High-performance computing applications increasingly depend on the
performance of parallel storage systems. IO500 is widely used to evaluate
these systems, but its performance strongly depends on many configuration
parameters. Finding a good configuration is difficult because a valid
IO500 execution is expensive, and an exhaustive search over all possible
configurations can require more than one year of computing time. This
internship studied how guided search methods can reduce this tuning cost.
The experiments were carried out with IOPS, a benchmark orchestration
framework used to generate configurations, execute IO500, collect results,
and cache previous measurements. To make the exhaustive study possible, a
reduced-cost IO500 setup was used, reducing the estimated cost from more
than one year to about 183 hours. The resulting dataset was then used to
compare Random Search and several Bayesian Optimization configurations.
The results show that Bayesian Optimization can reach approximately 98\%
of the best score with about 40--60 evaluations. Among the tested
configurations, Gaussian Process was the best surrogate model, while LCB
and EI gave similar results at the top. A comparison with a human
specialist also showed that expert tuning reached about 95\% of the
optimum, while Bayesian Optimization reached about 98\%. Finally, the
internship contributed to IOPS by adding IO500 support, updating its
documentation, and implementing TPE as an additional search method. These
results show that guided search is a promising way to reduce the cost of
IO500 tuning.
