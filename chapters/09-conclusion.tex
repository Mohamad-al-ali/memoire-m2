\subsection{Summary}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Problem: tuning IO500 properly is intractable because the validity
          rules make a single measurement cost $\approx$ \SI{30}{\minute}.
    \item Method: measure the whole grid once with deliberately \emph{invalid}
          but rank-preserving short runs, cache it, and replay every search
          strategy offline at zero cluster cost.
    \item Result: the exhaustive campaign cost $\approx \SI{183}{\hour}$
          instead of more than a year, and a well-chosen Bayesian Optimization
          setup reaches $\geq$ 98\,\% of the optimum in a few dozen
          evaluations, one order of magnitude cheaper than exhaustive search
          and above what random search or a human expert reached.
    \item Contribution to the tooling: the methodology, the configurations and
          the analysis scripts are packaged with IOPS and are reusable on
          another machine.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Perspectives}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Complete the \textbf{valid cluster validation} of the best
          configurations and report official-grade IO500 scores.
    \item Reproduce the study on \textbf{IRENE} (TGCC), a larger and more
          complex system, to test generalisation.
    \item Extend the \textbf{10-node challenge} study.
    \item Collect submissions from \textbf{more specialists} to make the human
          baseline statistically meaningful.
    \item Implement and compare \textbf{additional search techniques} against
          the existing cache: genetic algorithms, simulated annealing,
          greedy/coordinate search (to reproduce the CLAIX23 baseline
          directly on our data).
    \item Feed the diagnostic findings back into the IOPS
          \texttt{BayesianPlanner} (surrogate defaults, handling of discrete
          dimensions, duplicate-proposal policy).
    \item Longer term: transfer learning across machines -- reuse the response
          surface measured on one system to warm-start the search on another.
\end{itemize}
